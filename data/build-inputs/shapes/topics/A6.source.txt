  Revision notes
  • The specific: CVA as a spread (CR-20 c, i; S2). For pricing, an upfront CVA is often charged as a running
  spread. One simple way is to divide by the risky annuity (duration) of the maturity. This ignores the “CVA of
  the CVA”: charging CVA raises the trade value, which raises the CVA. The exact answer solves recursively;
  an xVA desk charging the first-order CVA always loses a little when the trade is booked. Gregory’s example:
  divide by risky annuity −1.92 bps, exact (recursive) −1.96, EPE approximation −2.01.
  • The average-EPE approximation (eq. 20.4): assuming constant EPE, UCVA = −average EPE × spread,
  in the units of the spread (for the maturity of the instrument). It uses the undiscounted EPE and has no LGD
  input: with LGDactual = LGDmkt the LGDs cancel to first order. It separates the credit component (spread)
  from the market risk component (average EPE).
  • Why CVA is hard (CR-20 a): a bond’s cash flow is fully at risk in default, but a swap’s cash flow is only
  partly at risk because it is partially offset by opposing flows; the fraction at risk depends on curve shape,
  forward rates and volatilities. CVA is now computed with market-implied (risk-neutral) parameters as an
  exit price (IFRS 13), although market-implied PDs are much higher than real-world ones, rarely hedgeable,
  and banks mostly warehouse credit risk.
  • CVA (CR-20 b): driven by three questions: does the counterparty default (PD), what is the exposure then
  (EPE), how much is lost (LGD = 1 − R). With independence (no wrong-way risk) only a single average
  LGD is needed and CVA becomes a weighted average of the EPE profile: market risk (EPE) times credit risk
  (PD). CVA is a negative adjustment. The PD in eq. 20.3 is the marginal PD for each interval.
  • Direct vs path-wise: the direct approach simulates default times and values the portfolio only at the default
  date; in the book’s 10-year swap it had a standard deviation six times smaller, a speed-up of 6 × 6 = 36
  times in valuations (Amdahl’s law limits the overall gain). It is less obviously applied to path-dependent
  (collateralised, exotic) portfolios.
  • Credit curve shape (CR-20 d): with the same five-year spread (150 bps), the upward-sloping curve gives
  the largest CVA on a 10-year receiver swap (−24.6 vs flat −20.0 vs inverted −15.7 bps), mainly from the
  largest extrapolated 10-year spread; defaults are back-loaded. CVA generally rises with the spread, but as
  default approaches it converges to the exposure (zero for a par trade): large gamma and jump-to-default risk.
  • LGD (CR-20 d): LGDmkt calibrates the PD from spreads; LGDactual is the loss actually expected. When equal,
  a higher LGD lowers the implied PD but raises the loss: 60% to 50% changes CVA by under 2%. A different
  LGDactual is justified by seniority (e.g. project finance, about 75% recovery vs about 40% for corporates),

  credit enhancements, or work-out experience. Avoid double counting: ratings mapped to spreads should
  reflect PD only (S&P definitions are PD-based; Moody’s may include recovery).
  • DVA (CR-20 e): the mirror of CVA using ENE, the party’s own PD and own LGD; it is positive because
  ENE is negative. Accounting drove it: FAS 157 (2006), IFRS 13 (2013). “My CVA is your DVA” (CVAA =
  −DVAB ) creates price symmetry. DVA is a double-edged sword: monetising it means defaulting, unwinds
  and novations (monolines, MBIA), close-out, or hedging by selling CDS on oneself (not possible), so banks
  sold protection on correlated names (some on Lehman). Basel derecognises DVA from capital; the FASB
  puts DVA in other comprehensive income; shareholders get nothing in default while bondholders may see
  DVA as real. Market practice treats DVA as a funding benefit (FVA).
  • UCVA vs BCVA (CR-20 f; S4): bilateral CVA and DVA include the survival probability of the other party
  up to the start of each interval (the “first-to-default” effect); UCVA and UDVA do not. The survival term
  makes CVA sensitive to one’s own credit quality (seen as unnatural; regulation may prohibit it). Default
  dependency and close-out (2002 ISDA lets the claim reflect the determining party’s creditworthiness) com-
  plicate it; Gregory and German (2013) find the formulas without survival probabilities probably the best
  simple approximation. Survey practice is split between contingent and non-contingent.
  • BCVA pricing intuition (CR-20 g; S5): with average EPE = −average ENE, BCVA = −EPE×(SpreadC −
  SpreadP ): the weaker party pays the stronger. In Table 20.3 the receive-fixed swap’s BCVA is close to zero
  (large ENE, so large DVA) and the OTM receive-fixed swap’s BCVA is positive (“paying through mid”).
  P
  • Netting (CR-20 h): netting can only reduce CVA: CVAN S ≥ i CVASA  i (less negative). It is captured by
  replacing standalone EPE with netting-set EPE, which needs Monte Carlo at the netting-set level; benefits
  are strong for balanced portfolios, weak for directional ones.
  • Incremental CVA (CR-20 i): netting-set CVA after the trade minus before. It is never more negative than
  standalone CVA, depends on trade order but not on later trades, and approaches standalone CVA as the new
  trade’s relative size grows (Figure 20.12: −6.2 bps small, towards −17.9 bps large). It also prices unwinds,
  restructurings and changes to margin terms. Incremental EPE (and DVA) can change sign for offsetting
  trades; with equal spreads BCVA has no incremental effect.
  • Marginal CVA (CR-20 i): uses marginal EPE; contributions sum to total CVA and are “fairer” (Table 20.5:
  IRS −17.7, XCCY −19.2, total −36.9). Not used to price new trades (it would re-price existing ones) but
  suited to trades executed together and to accounting allocation.

  Exam traps
  • Cumulative vs marginal PD (intermediate result): the CVA sum uses the PD for each interval, not the cu-
  mulative PD to each date. The survival bracket, by contrast, uses the other party’s cumulative PD to the start of
  the interval.
  • Wrong-input twin: recovery rate instead of LGD; the counterparty’s PD in the DVA leg (DVA uses the party’s
  own PD with ENE); the own spread on the EPE leg of the spread formula.
  • Sign convention: ENE is negative, so DVA is positive and BCVA = CVA + DVA. Subtracting DVA, or reporting
  CVA as a gain, flips the result.
  • The change is not the BCVA (intermediate result): when the move from UCVA to BCVA is asked, the answer
  is BCVA − UCVA; the BCVA itself and the DVA leg alone are the planted distractors.
  • Spread formula has no LGD: multiplying EPE × spread by LGD double counts it.
  • Incremental vs standalone vs total: the price of a new trade under netting is the incremental CVA, not its
  standalone CVA, not the whole netting set’s CVA, and not the netting benefit.
