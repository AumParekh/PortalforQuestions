  Revision notes
  • 1996 Amendment (ORR-21 c): two methods, standardized (five categories; specific and general market risk;
  positions assumed perfectly correlated) and internal models (preferred by banks with material trading books
  because charges were generally smaller). Internal
  √  models: 10-day VaR at the 99th percentile, at least one
  year of daily data, usually one-day VaR × 10; M R = max(V aRt−1 , m × V aRavg ) with V aRavg the
  60-day average and m ≥ 3. With m = 3 the second term is usually the larger.
  • Specific risk and Tier 3 (ORR-21 c): specific risk by internal model uses a multiplier of 4 and may not be
  below half the standardized charge (so internal-model banks also compute the standardized charge). Tier 3
  capital (unsecured subordinated debt, original maturity of at least two years) could meet about 70% of market
  risk capital. Qualitative criteria: independent risk management, limits, board involvement.
  • Backtesting and the multiplier (ORR-21 c): daily, one-day 99% VaR over the most recent 250 days; a day
  with a loss above VaR is an exception. Five or fewer exceptions: m = 3; more than five: larger at supervisory
  discretion; 10 or more: m = 4 required.
  • From capital to RWA (ORR-21 c): charges for specific and general market risk across the
  P five categories
  are summed and multiplied by 12.5 (the inverse of 8%), so trading capital = 0.08×12.5× j (M Rj +SRj )
  and the usual RWA multipliers apply.
  • Why stressed VaR (ORR-22 a): most banks used historical simulation; in calm periods measured VaR drifts
  down because nearly all observations are small, and when volatility returned in 2007 VaR was slow to follow.
  Basel 2.5 (implemented by end 2011) added stressed VaR, the incremental risk charge and the comprehensive
  risk charge.
  • Stressed VaR definition (ORR-22 a): draw daily observations from the one-year (250-day) period in the
  most recent seven years that was most stressful for the current portfolio. Result: a relatively large one-day
  VaR that changes little over time (unless calm persists for seven years); scaled to 10 days like VaR.
  • Basel 2.5 charge (ORR-22 a): M R2.5 = max(V aRt−1 , mc V aRavg ) + max(SV aRt−1 , ms SV aRavg ),
  both multipliers at least 3. Because the recent period cannot be more stressed than the stressed period and
  the two terms are summed, M R2.5 is at least twice the 1996 charge when the multipliers are equal. Each
  max is a separate decision: after a sharp position build-up the previous day’s VaR can exceed m times the
  average.
  • Incremental risk charge (ORR-22 b): banks had placed illiquid default-risky instruments in the trading
  book to exploit lower capital than the banking book. First an incremental default risk charge (internal model
  at the IRB 99.9th percentile, one-year horizon, or a standardized or current exposure approach; trading capital
  the greater of market risk and banking book capital). Then extended to rating changes, because most crisis
  credit losses came from downgrades, spreads and liquidity rather than defaults: positions downgraded or
  defaulted are replaced at the pre-downgrade rating (constant credit quality), with a liquidity horizon of at
  least three months.

  • Comprehensive risk charge (ORR-22 c): Basel II assumes a constant Gordy correlation, unreasonable
  for the correlation book (securitizations, re-securitizations, derivatives on them), whose tranche values move
  with correlation. The CR charge replaces the IRC and specific risk charge for the correlation book. Standard-
  ized capital as a % of exposure (not risk weights): securitizations 1.6%, 4%, 8%, 28%, 100% for AAA/AA,
  A, BBB, BB, below BB or unrated; re-securitizations 3.2%, 8%, 18%, 52%, 100%. Internal models need
  supervisory approval, a floor at a fraction of the standardized charge, and must capture multiple defaults and
  rating changes, correlation and spread volatility, basis risk, hedge dynamics and recovery volatility.

  Exam traps
  • The max is a decision (threshold): use V aRt−1 when it exceeds m × V aRavg ; always multiplying the average
  understates the charge after a spike.
  • Intermediate result: the VaR term alone is the 1996 charge; the Basel 2.5 charge adds the SVaR term.
  √
  • Unit conversion: one-day figures need 10; 10-day figures must not be scaled again.
  • Multiplier choice: four exceptions permit m = 3; 10 or more require 4. Do not apply 4 to a clean backtest.
  • Credit side, dropped term: subtract EL from the 99.9th percentile loss; the undeducted figure is Hull’s credit
  VaR, a planted intermediate.
  • CR table: the percentages are capital as a fraction of exposure, not risk weights; re-securitizations carry roughly
  double the securitization charge.
