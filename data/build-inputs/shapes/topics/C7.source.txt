  Revision notes
  • Expected discounted value is not price (MR-12 a): book tree, six-month rate 2% today, 2.50% or 1.50%
  in six months with true probability 0.5 each; one-year spot 2.15%. The one-year zero’s expected discounted
  value is 980.302, but its market price is 978.842: investors dislike the price uncertainty and penalise the risky
  bond.
  • Arbitrage pricing by replication (MR-12 b): a six-month call on a then six-month zero struck at 990 pays
  0 (up) or 2.556 (down). Solve F.5 + .987654F1 = 0, F.5 + .992556F1 = 2.556: long 521.4375 one-
  year zeros, short 515.0000 six-month zeros; price 0.504. Expected discounted value with true probabilities,
  1.2653, overstates it. The probabilities never enter the replication; they matter only indirectly through current
  bond prices.
  • Risk-neutral pricing (MR-12 c): find the probabilities that make expected discounted values equal mar-
  ket prices (p = 0.8009 recovers 978.842), then price any claim by expected discounted value under them:
  (0.1991 × 2.556)/1.01 = 0.504. It works because an arbitrage-priced security’s price does not depend on
  risk preferences; an imaginary risk-neutral economy with the same bond prices gives the same price.
  • True versus risk-neutral probabilities and drift (MR-12 d, S3): with true probabilities 0.5 the drift of the
  six-month rate is zero; under p = 0.8009 it is 0.8009(50) − 0.1991(50) = 30.09 bp. From date 1 to 2,
  q = 0.6520 gives a risk-neutral drift of 15.20 bp. Risk-neutral probabilities put more weight on the high-rate
  (low-price) states; MR-13 explains that a drift of a given number of basis points has the same pricing effect
  as a risk premium of that size per year of duration.
  • Multi-period extension (MR-12 e): add the 1.5-year zero (spot 2.25%, price 966.995) and solve for q at
  date 1 (assumed equal from both states). The tree then implies the one-year rate in six months (2.5754% or
  1.5754%): in a one-factor model the short-rate process determines every other rate. Replicating portfolios
  change by date and state: dynamic replication.
  • Recombining trees (MR-12 g): up-down lands on down-up. A nonrecombining tree has 2N end states (over
  500,000 for 10 years of semiannual steps, over 500 billion for 20), so it is computationally unwieldy; effects
  such as state-dependent volatility must be implemented more efficiently.
  • CMT swap (MR-12 h, S1): pays N (yCMT − K)/2 every six months, where yCMT is the semiannually com-
  pounded yield of a fixed maturity on the payment date (in practice 2-, 5- or 10-year Treasury yields; the book
  uses the six-month yield). Value at each date-1 node = expected discounted value of date-2 payoffs plus that
  node’s own date-1 payoff; then discount back to date 0.
  • Book CMT result: USD 1,000,000 struck at 2%: date-1 values 5,719.52 and −4, 227.29, date-0 value
  3,702.11. With true probabilities the value is close to zero (−6.07): the risk-neutral probabilities, not the
  true ones, determine the arbitrage price even though 2% is the average rate under the true probabilities.
  • Option-adjusted spread (MR-12 f, S2): the spread that, added to the model’s discount rates, recovers the
  market price. Rates are shifted only for discounting, never for computing cash flows. Book: an OAS of 10 bp

  fixed income derivatives for simplicity.

  Exam traps
  • True versus risk-neutral (wrong-input twin): the real-world probabilities (often 0.5) give the wrong price.
  Only the risk-neutral probabilities recover market prices.
  • OAS shifts discounting only: adding the OAS to the rates that set the CMT payments changes the cash flows
  and inflates the value.
  • Cheap versus rich (polarity flip): a positive OAS means the market price is below the model value: cheap. A
  negative OAS means rich.
  • Dropped term: each date-1 node value includes that node’s own payment as well as the discounted date-2
  expectation.
  • Same probability at every date: q at date 1 is solved separately (book 0.6520 versus 0.8009 at date 0); reusing
  p is a named mistake.
  • Intermediate result: the model value at zero OAS is a legitimate step, not the market price when an OAS is
  quoted.
