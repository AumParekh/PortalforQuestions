  • Credit spread defined (CR-9 h): “the extra rate of interest per annum required by investors for bearing a
  particular credit risk”. Measures: CDS spread, bond yield spread (over a similar risk-free bond), asset swap
  spread. Buying a 7% bond plus 200 bps protection approximates a 5% risk-free bond, so the n-year CDS
  spread ≈ the n-year par yield spread.
  • Which risk-free rate: traders quote over Treasuries; rates implied from CDS and bonds are higher than
  Treasuries, near AA short-term borrowing rates (bond 4.7%, CDS 80 bps implies 3.9%). Asset swap spread:
  its PV equals the risk-free bond price minus the corporate bond price.
  • CDS-bond basis = CDS spread − bond yield spread. Drivers: bond price away from par (above par negative,

  interest in the payoff (negative); restructuring clause (positive); a different risk-free reference. Positive pre-
  2007 (about 16 bps), very negative in the crisis, small and negative since.

  • Real-world versus spread-implied (CR-9 j, k): seven-year hazard rates for A-rated bonds: 0.109% from de-
  fault history versus 1.145% from a 68.68 bps spread; the gap widens as quality falls (CCC/C: 10.118% versus
  18.395%). Causes: illiquidity (about 25 bps), traders’ depression scenarios, and above all systematic risk
  from correlated defaults, plus undiversifiable skewed idiosyncratic risk. Use spread-implied (risk-neutral)
  PDs for valuation, historical (real-world) PDs for scenario analysis.
  • Credit spread risk (CR-10 f): trading book credit products depend on spreads, so VaR or ES must capture

  Exam traps
  • Polarity flip (S1): ln(Dt /F ) is negative for risky debt, so the yield needs the minus sign. A negative “spread”
  on risky debt is the card’s inverted formula at work.
  • Intermediate result: − T1 ln(Dt /F ) is the yield; the spread needs −r.
  • Annualising: forgetting 1/T reports a T -year cumulative log loss as an annual spread.
  • Wrong-input twin: PD divides by LGD = 1 − R, not by R.
  • Spread is not PD: the spread (or 1 − (1 + rf )/(1 + y)) is an expected loss rate; ranking issuers by spread
  ignores recovery and can reverse the PD ranking.
  • Risk-neutral versus real-world: spread-implied PDs are risk-neutral and much higher than historical ones; use
  them for valuation and CVA, not for scenario analysis.
