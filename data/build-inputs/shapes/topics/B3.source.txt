  Revision notes
  • Default correlation (CR-11 a, S1). Two firms, default probabilities π1 , π2 over a horizon T , joint default
  probability π12 . Defaults are Bernoulli variables: no default 1 − π1 − π2 + π12 ; firm 1 only π1 − π12 ; firm
  2 only π2 − π12 ; both π12 . π12 is “a genuinely new parameter, a primitive”: it cannot be computed from π1
  and π2 unless defaults are independent.
  • Moments (CR-11 a). Means πi ; variances πi (1 − πi ); covariance π12 − π1 π2 ; correlation = covariance over
  the product of standard deviations (eq. 11.1). Independence gives π12 = π1 π2 and ρ12 = 0; the larger the
  excess of π12 over π1 π2 , the higher the correlation. P (at least one) = π1 + π2 − π12 .
  • Example 11.1. π1 = 0.0025, π2 = 0.0125: independent π12 = 0.000031 (under a third of a basis point);
  with ρ = 5%, π12 = 0.000309, “nearly 10 times as great”.
  • Order of magnitude (CR-11 a). Default is rare, so default correlation is hard to estimate from default data;
  most studies find one-year correlations around 0.05, varying widely by period, industry and domicile, and
  often negative. Because PDs are small, an “optically” small correlation can have a large impact.
  • Drawbacks of the correlation framework (CR-11 b). With n > 2 credits there are 2n events but only
  n + 1 + n(n − 1)/2 conditions (3 credits: 8 versus 7; 10 credits: 1,024 versus 56), so pairwise correlations
  cannot specify the full distribution. Many pairwise parameters are needed, and a single common parameter
  must be non-negative to keep the correlation matrix positive-definite. Guarantees and revolvers (option-like),
  CDS basis trades (technical factors) and convertibles (equity and vega risk) fit the framework poorly.
  p

  ai is standard normal, beta to the factor is βi , and the asset return correlation of any pair is βi βj (Example
  11.3: cyclical 0.5, defensive 0.1, correlation 0.05). Firm i defaults if ai ≤ ki , with πi = Φ(ki ).

  • Purpose of copulas (MR-10 a). A copula joins univariate marginal distributions into one multivariate dis-
  tribution, C : [0, 1]n → [0, 1] (Sklar 1959). One-parameter: Gaussian (strictly, only the bivariate one)
  and Archimedean (Gumbel, Clayton, Frank); two-parameter: Student’s t, Fréchet, Marshall-Olkin. Vasicek
  brought one-factor copulas to finance (1987), Li the multivariate default-time copula (2000); embraced, then
  “fell into disgrace” in the 2007 crisis.

  • Implied correlation (CR-22 l). Backed out of standard tranche prices once risk-neutral PDs from single-
  name CDS are in: the unique copula correlation that makes the modelled tranche value match the market
  price. Associated with a tranche’s attachment point (base correlation); it varies by tranche (correlation skew).
  Risk-neutral; a market rather than credit risk concept. See D15 and D14 (compound versus base correlation).
  • Six correlation concepts (CR-22). Default correlation; asset return correlation (Merton-type models, driven
  by factor loadings); equity return correlation (proxy for unobservable asset correlation); copula correlation
  (no direct economic interpretation; the Gaussian copula correlation equals the Gaussian single-factor cor-
  relation); spread correlation (mark-to-market); implied credit correlation (copula correlation inferred from
  prices).

  Exam traps
  • Dropped term: omitting π1 π2 from the numerator of ρ12 (overstates it), or omitting +π1 π2 when rebuilding
  π12 (understates it).
  p  √
  • Denominator: the standard deviations are πi (1 − πi ), not πi ; the gap matters for high-yield PDs.
  • Sibling swap, correlations: asset (or equity) return correlation βi βj is not default correlation; for π = 1%,
  0.315 asset correlation is only 0.05 default correlation.
  • Sibling swap, outcomes: “at least one” is π1 + π2 − π12 ; “exactly one” subtracts π12 twice.
  • Polarity flip, conditional PD: in a downturn (m < 0) a higher beta means a higher conditional PD.
  • Scope: the Gaussian copula makes the transformed variables xi normal, not the default times; implied correla-
  tion is risk-neutral and market-based, not a historical estimate.
