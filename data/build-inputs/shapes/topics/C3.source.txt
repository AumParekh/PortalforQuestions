  Revision notes
  • Why mapping (MR-5 a): “Mapping is the process by which the current values of the portfolio positions are
  replaced by exposures on the risk factors.” VaR is aggregation at the highest level (J.P. Morgan Chase: 2.1
  million positions, 240,000 pricing series). Thousands of USD/EUR forwards collapse onto one risk factor,
  the spot rate: acceptable for risk measurement, not for pricing.
  • Mapping as a data solution (MR-5 a): instruments whose profile changes as they age (bonds must be
  mapped on yields that represent their current profile; options on their primary risk factors); positions with no
  history (IPOs replaced by similar risk factors); stale prices across time zones (Tokyo closes 15 hours before
  New York), which make daily cross-market correlations too low.
  • The process (MR-5 a): mark every position to market in the reference currency, allocate each market value
  to the risk factors (any unallocated part is cash, which has no risk), then sum exposures by factor into the
  vector x. Allocation is exact for derivatives priced off the factors (partial derivatives) and estimated for others
  (a stock’s regression beta on the index).

  • Three fixed-income methods (MR-5 c; S1): principal mapping uses one risk factor at the average portfolio
  maturity (redemption timing only); duration mapping uses one risk factor at the portfolio duration; cash-
  flow mapping groups the portfolio cash flows into maturity buckets (vertices). “Mapping should preserve
  the market value of the position. Ideally, it also should preserve its market risk.”
  • Jorion’s two-bond example (MR-5 c, d; S3): USD 100 million 5-year 6% plus USD 100 million 1-year
  4%, both at par; average maturity 3 years, duration 2.733 years. Principal VaR = 200 × 1.484% = USD
  2.97 million (“overstates the true risk because it ignores intervening coupon payments”). Duration VaR:
  interpolated risk 0.987 + (1.484 − 0.987)(0.733) = 1.351%, USD 2.70 million. Cash flow: undiversified
  USD 2.63 million, diversified USD 2.57 million.
  • Decomposing the gap (MR-5 c): of the USD 130,000 between duration (2.70) and diversified cash-flow
  (2.57) VaR, USD 70,000 comes from risk not being linear in maturity (2.70 versus 2.63) and USD 60,000
  from correlations below one (2.63 versus 2.57). Ranking by fidelity: cash flow best, then duration, principal
  crudest.

  index (duration 4.62): absolute monthly 95% VaR USD 1.99 million on USD 100 million. A duration-

  • Variance improvement (MR-5 f; S2): 1 − (TE-VaR/VaRindex )2 = 1 − (0.43/1.99)2 = 95.4%, “similar to
  an R2 in a regression” and “in line with the explanatory power of the first factor”. The remaining tracking
  error “is due to nonparallel moves in the term structure”.
  • Absolute is not relative (MR-5 f): Jorion’s portfolios 2 to 5 have TE-VaR 0.29, 0.16, 0.20, 0.81. Portfolio
  3 (2- and 9-year) tracks best because it comes closest to the index cash flows; portfolio 5 (1-month bills
  and 30-year zeros, a barbell) has the lowest absolute VaR (1.71) but the worst tracking error. “Minimizing
  absolute market risk is not the same as minimizing relative market risk”; duration hedging is only a first
  approximation.
  • Linear derivatives (MR-5 g): long currency forward = long foreign currency spot + long foreign currency
  bill + short USD bill (EUR 100 million example: USD 125.89 million each; spot VaR 5.713, total 5.735).
  Commodity forwards map on the forward price (convenience yield is not tied to another financial variable):
  12-month oil, USD 43.743 million × 14.05% = USD 6.146 million. Long 6 × 12 FRA = long 6-month bill
  + short 12-month bill (VaR 0.327). A swap is a fixed bond against an FRN (FRN on cash just before reset)
  or a string of forwards; VaR USD 2.152 million either way, 1.763 after the first reset.
  • Options (MR-5 g): delta-normal mapping: long call = long ∆ of the asset + short (∆S − c) in a bill (ATM:
  ∆ = 0.536, c = 4.20, asset 53.60, loan 49.40). Delta is not constant; a linear approximation is acceptable
  for long-dated options over short risk horizons. Deep in the money, the option behaves like a forward.

  Exam traps
  • Principal mapping polarity: it overstates risk (ignores intervening coupons), so it gives the highest VaR of the
  three.
  • Duration versus cash flow (sibling swap): duration mapping uses one vertex at the portfolio duration; only
  cash-flow mapping spreads each payment over the grid and captures imperfect correlation.
  • Variance improvement, not VaR improvement: square the ratio; 1 − TE-VaR/VaR understates the improve-
  ment.
  • Absolute versus relative risk: the barbell (cash and a 30-year zero) has the lowest absolute VaR and the worst
  tracking error; matching absolute VaR to the index is not tracking.
  • Market value is preserved by all three methods (scope error): what distinguishes them is how well they
  preserve market risk.
