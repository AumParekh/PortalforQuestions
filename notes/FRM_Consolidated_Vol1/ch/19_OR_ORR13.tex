\chapterhead{52}{ORR-13}{Case Study: Third-Party Risk Management}{1}{3}

\lohead{52}{a}{Explain how risks related to the use of third parties can arise and
describe characteristics of an effective third-party risk management framework.}

\orsub{Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM)}

TPRM addresses risks from outsourcing to third parties, including vendors,
service providers, and business partners.

\begin{orkeybox}
{\sffamily\bfseries\fontsize{8.6}{10}\selectfont\color{navy}Motivations}\par
\vspace{2pt}
Cost savings and risk mitigation by leveraging specialised expertise.
\end{orkeybox}

\orsubb{Key risks in third-party engagements}
\begin{itemize}
\item Include service disruptions, fraud, data breaches, IP theft, and even
espionage.
\item Outsourcing core processes increases these risks, necessitating robust
TPRM.
\end{itemize}

\orsubb{Why third-party use has accelerated}
\begin{itemize}
\item IoT adoption and the COVID-19 pandemic have increased reliance on third
parties.
\item Significant data breach rates through third parties highlight the need for
effective TPRM.
\end{itemize}

\lohead{52}{b}{Describe the lessons learned from the case study involving a data
breach caused by a third-party vendor.}

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.32}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.15\textwidth}YY@{}}
\rowcolor{navy} \thd{} & \thd{Capital One breach} & \thd{Morgan Stanley incident}\\
{\tabfont \tsub{What happened}} &
{\tabfont A former AWS employee exploited a weakness in the cloud storage
firewall to access unencrypted Capital One data.} &
{\tabfont Improper decommissioning of data servers and inadequate oversight of
third-party vendors involved in the process.}\\
\rowcolor{paleblue}
{\tabfont \tsub{Consequences}} &
{\tabfont Data breach of millions of customers' information. The bank was fined
\$80 million for inadequate vendor risk management.} &
{\tabfont Confidential customer data remained on disposed hardware, violating
trust. Fined \$60 million for deficient risk management related to vendors.}\\
{\tabfont \tsub{Failures}} &
{\tabfont
$\blacktriangleright$~Both Capital One and AWS were aware of the firewall
vulnerability.\newline
$\blacktriangleright$~Unencrypted data allowed the stolen information to be
readily usable.\newline
$\blacktriangleright$~Capital One failed to properly assess and manage risks
associated with using AWS.} &
{\tabfont
$\blacktriangleright$~Failed to assess and address risks related to hardware
decommissioning.\newline
$\blacktriangleright$~Lacked proper vetting and monitoring of the third-party
vendors involved.\newline
$\blacktriangleright$~Did not maintain a proper inventory of customer data.}\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{minipage}
\orfigcap{Two third-party failures, compared side by side.}

\begin{orexambox}{Lessons learned from both cases}
\begin{lettered}
\item You are responsible for your data security, despite cloud advantages
(Capital One).
\item Strong vendor risk management: assess and monitor vendors to avoid risks
(Morgan Stanley).
\item Encrypt data to safeguard it, even in breaches (Capital One).
\item Manage and mitigate risks with vendors handling sensitive data (Morgan
Stanley).
\end{lettered}
\end{orexambox}
